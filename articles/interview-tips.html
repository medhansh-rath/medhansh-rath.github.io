Practical Tips & Real Advice for Tech Interviews

A casual, honest breakdown of how student/working student interviews actually go, how to handle technical questions, and what to ask at the end.

Download HTML

Hey, so here are some tips for the interview. First of all, the most important thing is to be honest. If they ask you something technical and you don't know it, then just say that you don't know it. Or if you have a vague feeling, you can say something like, "I'm not super familiar with the subject, but it might be X and Y." (Don't use those exact words, but you get the point of what you need to convey).

"Make the stuff you've done seem important, but not too much. It's fine if you're 100% honest, but a little strategic positioning goes a long way."

This honesty rule, however, doesn't strictly apply when you are talking about yourself—it's okay to exaggerate a bit when talking about your experience or weaknesses. Don't go overboard, keep it around 90% honest and 10% slight exaggerations (not flat-out lies).

For instance, in an internship I did, I basically set up some small test automation just to get a feel for the field—none of it was actually used by the main team. But I usually don't mention that last part. Basically, make the work you've done sound meaningful and relevant without over-claiming.

How the Interview Usually Flows

1. Introductions: They introduce themselves and spend ~2 minutes talking about who they are and what they do.
2. Your Turn: You give your introduction—make sure to have a solid elevator pitch ready!
3. Standard & Technical Questions: They ask about your strengths/weaknesses and technical questions based on the job. (For AI roles: applications of AI, what optimization is, etc.). Don't bluff if you don't know—it's totally fine.
4. Project Overview: They describe the project, scope of work, and daily responsibilities.
5. Q&A Phase: Time for you to ask them questions. Always have questions ready!

Questions to Ask at the End

Even if you feel like you have no questions at the end, always think of something to ask—it shows initiative. Here are a few questions I like to ask:

  • "Could you tell me a bit more about the team I will be working with?"
  • "Do you currently have interns or working students on the team? What are some of the main challenges previous students faced in this role?"

Preparation Strategy

Read the job description carefully and look up terms or concepts you don't know yet. They won't deduct points for you not knowing every niche detail, but if the job mentions something like "AI methods for additive manufacturing," it's a huge plus to understand the basics of what additive manufacturing even is.

They know you are a student—they don't expect you to be a master of everything, so don't stress out too much!
